Audit and roadmapA page-by-page audit against Zoom, a gap analysis and a phased roadmap. Details below.

Audit and roadmap

QuestionWhich pages and experiences does the Vconnct site lack or underdevelop compared with a mature video-meeting site, and what should change first?
MethodPage-by-page comparison of site structure (products, features, solutions, resources, about, download, support, pricing, help, trust, developer platform), then a comparison across content, design, performance, calls to action, pricing UX, trust signals, developer visibility and support. A Figma design was made for the key pages.
Main gaps foundNo Features pages. Thin Resources and About sections. Industry and audience pages with low visibility. Pricing shown as flat blocks. Developer docs buried in the footer. Generic calls to action. No dedicated testimonial or trust section.
Strengths to promoteThe live chatbot and help center, regional focus (for example the Ahl al Quran audience), AI transcription and translation, six plans across two products.

Prioritised roadmap

  • Phase 1, quick wins: chatbot icon and support badge in the header, sharper and sticky calls to action, a pricing comparison toggle between the two products.
  • Phase 2, mid-term: a Solutions tab with use-case landing pages, and a top-navigation Developers hub with SDKs and guides.
  • Phase 3, long-term: a "Trusted By" section with logos and testimonials.

I used AI assistance for this audit. I structured the work, checked that each page and item exists, and added what was missing or removed extras. The audit is my analysis and recommendations. It does not claim any of the roadmap shipped or changed results.

Product research and decisions

Two research pieces I wrote for Vconnct. Each one states the question, the criteria, the comparison and my recommendation. These are my recommendations. Only the adoption noted below is confirmed.

1. Whiteboard for the meeting engine

QuestionWhich open-source whiteboard should sit inside the Flutter-based meeting engine for a Zoom-like experience?
Options comparedExcalidraw, tldraw, WBO (Whitebophir), Linwood Butterfly
CriteriaIntegration approach in Flutter, real-time collaboration, features, extensibility, licensing (MIT, AGPL, tldraw watermark), community, resilience to network drops, meeting needs such as presenter and viewer roles and breakout rooms
Recommendation1. Excalidraw, embedded through a WebView. 2. tldraw. 3. WBO. 4. Linwood Butterfly.
  • Excalidraw ranked first for mature real-time collaboration, a permissive MIT license and a large community. The cost is a WebView embed and handling roles in the app.
  • tldraw is the most extensible but needs a watermark or a paid license.
  • Linwood Butterfly is Flutter-native but built for personal notes, with experimental collaboration and an AGPL license.

2. Payment and billing stack

QuestionWhich modular, open-source-first stack could let Vconnct take payments and run subscriptions globally and in the Middle East, with less vendor lock-in?
Options comparedHyperswitch, Lago, Geidea, UniPay
CriteriaSubscription management, global and regional payment support, invoicing, security and compliance, integration options, scalability, admin usability, licensing, community, rough cost
RecommendationHyperswitch for global payment orchestration, Lago for billing and subscriptions, Geidea for Middle East methods (Mada, Apple Pay). UniPay as an optional all-in-one, flagged as costly.
  • Each tool covers a different layer, so the case for a modular stack is that no single option covered orchestration, billing and regional payments.
  • I added an integration note on how Zitadel for sign-in could connect to these tools for a B2B admin portal and for the B2C Super App, including webhook handling.
  • Of the tools I recommended, Lago was the one adopted by the company (per my confirmation). Hyperswitch and Geidea stay as recommendations.
  • Cost comparisons in the source document are my own rough estimates and are not shown as figures here.

Super App: scoping, design and a change of direction

The Super App was an all-in-one app: chat, calls, games and payment transactions. It did not launch.

ProblemCommunication is fragmented. Users get lost across many mobile apps. The Super App combined messages, calls, sending money, games and more in one place.
First phaseMessages and calls. These were already built and ready before the decision to change the technology stack.
What I didDefined what to build, chose which features to start with, designed the product myself, and followed up with the development team.
What happenedAfter development and design were done, management decided to change the whole technology stack because the existing one did not support scaling.
What I handledI told the team after they had finished, persuaded them toward the new technology, and managed their frustration that completed work would be redone.
ResultThe Super App lost priority in the company's plans after several execution blockers with the team. It did not launch.

What I learned

  • Be flexible. Search and experiment a lot, because not everything goes as first planned.
  • Technology can evolve and be replaced.
  • How to handle a tired team when I have to tell them a big part of their work will be redone.
  • People follow a manager they like in order to carry out what is wanted, more than one who just gives orders. This is the biggest thing I learned from all of my time at Vconnct.
