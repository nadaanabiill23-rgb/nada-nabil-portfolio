Case Study 09 | Vconnct Website

Owning the company website, from research and a CMS plan to nine releases.

The Vconnct website is where the company shows its products, pricing, help and legal information. I worked on it as designer and Associate Product Manager.

Role: Associate Product Manager / DesignerCompany website, SaaSVconnct
Challenge

A website with gaps against competitors, uneven secondary pages, content managed through developers, and a team that changed often.

Approach

Compare with the market, plan a CMS, redesign page by page, ship in versioned releases and report every week.

Scope

Research, requirements, redesign user stories, design, release notes, sprint and weekly reporting.

Status

Nine releases documented, 5.7.0 to 6.5.0.

What was released

  • Rebuilt the site on Next.js in version 6.0.0, keeping the same user journey and adding a new landing page and components.
  • Added a Trust Center with dynamic content for terms, privacy and policies (6.1.0), then search and updated certificates (6.4.0).
  • Reworked the free trial: the button adapts to the user, with tailored pop-ups (6.1.0). Added a phone number pop-up after sign-in and a thank-you page with campaign tracking (6.2.0).
  • Redesigned the homepage and navigation bar (6.3.0). Split landing pages into a separate project so marketing pages deploy on their own.
  • Published a page for each product and a download page (6.4.0). Added a feature page per product with a "Submit a Feature Idea" section, an About Us page and a Contact Us page (6.5.0).
  • Worked in Arabic and English, including the Arabic label for the Trust Center.

Compare with Zoom firstThe comparison listed gaps in content, design consistency on secondary pages and performance, and gave a recommended action for each, grouped into a 90-day roadmap.
Plan a CMS for non-developersResearch compared CMS options. The PRD specified a CMS so teams can create pages, publish legal documents and run a Knowledge Hub without a developer. Later release notes show content managed from a CMS, for example the Trust Center and the homepage announcement bar.
Write the redesign as user storiesThe redesign epic has user stories with acceptance criteria for the home, About Us, features, glossary, video library and comparison pages, and lists what was still missing: pricing, contact, Trust Center, support and blog.
Assess tools before adopting themThe Framer report weighed workflow, CMS collections, cost and add-ons, and recommended a three-month trial with a small team before any wider move.
Plan what to measureA tracking matrix lists page engagement, clicks, funnels from "Book a Demo" and sign-up, and campaign tracking. Release 6.2.0 added campaign tracking scripts to the thank-you page.
Keep marketing pages separateLanding pages became a separate project in 6.3.0, so campaigns can change faster with less risk to the main site.
